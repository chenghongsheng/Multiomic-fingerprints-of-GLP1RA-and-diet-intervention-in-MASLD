# Complementary control of lipid flux by semaglutide and dietary reversion in MASLD
This repository contains the R scripts for the multiomic analysis. The study aims to explore the shared and distinct mechanisms of dietary modification and glucogon-like-peptide 1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RA) in metabolic dysfunction associated steatotic liver disease.

## Overview
Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and related metabolic disorders involve complex systemic interactions across multiple organs, yet effective therapies remain limited. Although glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) and dietary interventions show promise, their distinct systemic impacts on key metabolic tissues, including the liver, white adipose tissue (WAT), and gut, remain poorly understood. We employed comprehensive multiomic analyses in a translational preclinical MASLD model to elucidate how semaglutide (a GLP-1RA) and dietary intervention achieve therapeutic benefits. Our findings reveal unique yet overlapping mechanisms. Semaglutide modulates hepatic circadian regulators, notably the Bmal1-bile acid synthetic enzymes axis, to suppress bile acid synthesis, reduce intestinal lipid absorption, and prevent ectopic fat deposition. In contrast, dietary intervention broadly enhances lipid utilization, drives extensive WAT reprogramming, reshapes gut microbiome composition, and increases bile acid deconjugation by beneficial taxa. Both treatments markedly mitigate inflammation, oxidative stress, and fibrosis, extending metabolic improvement beyond the liver. By clarifying distinct yet complementary multi-organ mechanisms underlying GLP-1RA therapy and dietary intervention, our study provide a novel framework for integrative treatment strategies targeting liver and WAT metabolism and gut health. These findings offer insights supporting personalized or combined therapeutic strategies to effectively manage diverse metabolic dysfunctions in MASLD.
